# Partner Outreach — turning a live lead into a renting business

This is Phase 4 of [LAUNCH-CHECKLIST.md](LAUNCH-CHECKLIST.md), written out. Use it
the moment a site produces its first real lead — that lead is the single best
asset you will ever have for signing a partner, and it decays in hours.

## The core move

Do not cold-pitch "want to buy leads from my website?" Every trade in Australia
gets that call weekly and it reads as a scam.

Pitch the opposite: **you already have their customer, and you're giving them
away.** A free live lead is unfalsifiable proof, it costs you nothing, and it
inverts the power dynamic — you're not selling, they're auditioning.

## Sequencing

1. **Customer first.** Call the lead, qualify, set the expectation that a couple
   of local operators will ring them. Never leave a lead sitting while you go
   find someone to service it.
2. **Same day, contact 3–4 operators.** Not one. You are running a trial with a
   control group, and the one who calls back fastest is your partner.
3. **Send the same lead to 2–3 of them.** This is fine and normal — the customer
   asked for multiple quotes, and your site promises exactly that.
4. **Score them.** Did they call the customer back the same day? Did they turn up?
   Did they quote in writing? A trade who is slow with a free lead will be slower
   with a paid one. Cut them.
5. **Convert after 2–3 free leads,** not after one. Let them feel the pipeline.

## Who to approach, in order

1. **Already running Google/Facebook ads** for this service in this town. They
   have a customer-acquisition budget and already know what a lead costs them.
   This is by far the best group.
2. **Map pack, few reviews, no real website.** Hungry, and your site solves a
   problem they know they have.
3. **Established with a good website.** Lowest priority — they have their own
   flow and will haggle hardest.

Avoid: franchises (no local authority to sign), and anyone who can't answer
their own phone.

## Script — phone call (best channel, use this first)

> "Hi, is that [name]? My name's Travis — I run bendigoestatecleanouts.com, it's a
> local site people find when they search for estate clearances in Bendigo.
>
> I've got a family in [suburb] who need a two-bedroom unit cleared. I'm not a
> clearance company, I just get the enquiries — so I'm looking for someone local
> to send them to.
>
> Can I text you their details now? No charge, no catch. If it turns into a job,
> great. If it goes well I've got more coming through and we can talk about
> making it regular."

**If they ask what's in it for you:** be straight. "Eventually I'd like to charge
a flat monthly fee for the leads from that site — but I'm not asking for
anything until you've had a few and seen whether they're any good."

**If they say "just send them through":** perfect. Send immediately, then follow
up the next morning to ask whether they got hold of the customer. That follow-up
call is the real test.

## Script — SMS (after the call, or if they don't pick up)

> Hi [name], Travis here — I run bendigoestatecleanouts.com. Got a family in
> [suburb] needing a 2br unit cleared, happy to pass them straight to you free.
> Interested? No catch, just looking for a local I can send these to.

Short. No links (links in a cold SMS read as spam). No pricing talk.

## Script — email (weakest channel; use only if there's no phone)

Subject: **Estate clearance enquiry in [suburb] — free, if you want it**

> Hi [name],
>
> I run bendigoestatecleanouts.com — a local site that comes up when people
> search for deceased estate clearances around Bendigo.
>
> I'm not a clearance company. I just get the enquiries and I don't currently
> have anyone local to send them to. There's one in [suburb] right now — a
> two-bedroom unit, family wants it cleared in the next couple of weeks.
>
> If you want it, reply or call me on [number] and I'll send the details across.
> No charge and nothing to sign — I'd just rather it go to someone local than sit
> in my inbox.
>
> Travis
> [number]

## Forwarding the lead

Forward the **Job details** box from the lead email verbatim — it's built to be
forwarded, and it's the block they price from. Add one line of context:

> "Spoke to her this morning. Husband's mother passed last month, she's the
> executor, keys are with the agent. Wants it done before it's listed."

That single line is what makes you a partner rather than a lead broker.

## Converting to rent

Once they've had 2–3 leads and closed at least one:

> "You've had [N] through now and closed [the Eaglehawk one]. I'd like to make
> that exclusive — you'd be the only clearance company the site sends to, and I'd
> stop sending them to anyone else. [$X] a month, cancel any time, first month
> free if you want to run it out further."

**Pricing anchor:** work backwards from job value, not from your costs. If their
average estate cleanout is $1,500 and they close 1 in 3 leads, each lead is worth
~$500 to them. Ten leads a month is $5,000 of work — a $500–800/month flat fee is
an easy yes and still leaves them well ahead.

- **Flat monthly via Stripe autopay** — strongly preferred. Predictable, no
  disputes, no visibility into their books required.
- **Rev-share (10–20%)** — only if they'll give you read access to their CRM.
  Without verification you will be underpaid, every time.

**Test them before you commit to exclusivity.** Exclusive means your site's
entire output depends on one business answering their phone.

## Record it

When an arrangement is agreed, write it into the site's `notes` field in
[sites.json](sites.json): partner name, contact, terms, start date. That's what
makes it visible to Hermes and to future-you.

## While you have no partner (manual middleman mode)

Perfectly viable, and it's where every site starts:

- Answer every lead yourself, same day. Speed beats polish.
- Never quote a price. Point at the published range and let the operator quote.
- Keep a simple record of every lead and what happened to it — that history is
  your negotiating evidence at rent time ("the site did 14 enquiries last month").
- Get a real phone number on the site as soon as there's volume. Form-only
  costs you the customers who want to talk to a human right now, which in
  bereavement niches is most of them.
